# Contracts

JSON Schemas for data that one part of MobSimLab produces and another consumes. Each schema names its producer and is checked in CI against real output.

| Schema | Producer | Consumer | Check |
|--------|----------|----------|-------|
| [rail-topology.schema.json](rail-topology.schema.json) | `crates/rail-design` (`topology` module; the railway tool's **Download track topology**) | Railway operations (ADR-0032): routes, timetables, reservations | `cargo run -p rail-design --example topology -- DIR`, then `tools/contracts/check_rail_topology.py DIR/*.json` |
| [level-crossing-closures.schema.json](level-crossing-closures.schema.json) | `crates/rail-plan` (`closures` module; `--example level_crossing_closures`) | Street line tool: expected wait at level crossings in bus running times | `crates/rail-plan/tests/closures.rs`, `crates/street-routing/src/crossings.rs` |
| [published-timetable.schema.json](published-timetable.schema.json) | `crates/rail-plan` (`journeys` module; `cargo run -p rail-plan --example publish_journeys -- TOPOLOGY LINES`), the web app's street lines | Journey planner (ADR-0034): `crates/journey-planner`, the panel's **Add rail timetable (JSON)** | `cargo test -p rail-plan --test journeys` |
| [plugin-manifest.schema.json](plugin-manifest.schema.json), [plugin-mode.schema.json](plugin-mode.schema.json) | Plugin authors; the bundled plugins in `plugins/` (ADR-0036) | `tools/plugins/check.py`, which writes the web app's registry `apps/web/src/plugins/bundled.generated.json` | `tools/plugins/check.py --check-rendered` |

## Railway topology

The railway design (ADR-0030, ADR-0033) is exported as a graph of physical tracks:

- **Sections**: one track between two nodes. These are the lines of an alignment (cut at switch points), station tracks and the diverging legs of switches. Each section carries its length, mode, gauge, use (running or storage), electrification, and ranges of level, permitted speed, ramp gradient and curve radius along it. It also gives its train protection (`line_of_sight`, `fixed_block`, or moving block with `cbtc` or `etcs_l2`), and lists its platform edges and its geometry.
- **Nodes**: where section ends meet. A node is `end` (a buffer stop or unconnected end), `join` or `switch`.
- **Links**: the movements a train may make through a node. At a switch point a train goes from the toe side into either leg, never from one leg into the other.
- **Switches**: catalogue element, diverging speed and radius, and the conflict sections that one train at a time may occupy. **Fouling points** say, for each section attached at a switch point, how far from the node a train is clear of the switch's other routes: where the track centres are the clearance marker distance apart. The point can lie beyond a short leg, on the section joined to it, so each fouling point also gives that section and the offset on it. The approach to a switch point fouls neither route (0 m). Detection sections and switch reservations need each section only up to its fouling point, not the whole line section.
- **Platform edges**: station, station name, side, and the stretch of the station track they serve.
- **Crossings** of two alignments, with each track's chainage and the fouling points before and after. The fouling distance is (c + e_other/2) / sin θ + (e_own/2) / tan θ, where c is the larger clearance marker distance of the two systems, e the track spacing and θ the crossing angle. A same-level crossing (`diamond`) is a conflict for one train at a time from either railway system. A grade-separated crossing is not a conflict. A `clash` is a design error.
- **Level crossings**: where street-level track crosses a street from OpenStreetMap (id `lc{track}-{way}-{n}`). Each lists the track, its chainage and the line sections the street covers, the line speed, the warning time and the approach distance (warning time × line speed) at which an approaching train closes the street, barriers or traffic signals, and the street: OSM way, name, class, width, whether buses use it or a trolleybus wire runs along it, and the distance from the crossing point to each stop line. The rail zone is w/2 / sin θ + h / tan θ and the stop line distance (h + s) / sin θ + w/2 / tan θ, where w is the carriageway width, h half the track's swept width (half the track spacing plus half the clearance marker distance), s the road stop clearance and θ the angle between track and street. A level crossing is a conflict between trains and road vehicles. The railway owns it (ADR-0007): it closes the street before a train and clears the train only when the street is closed, and road vehicles never stop between the stop lines. `standard` is false, and `special_measures` says why, where the crossing is outside the usual rules: a railway system that normally has no level crossings, a third rail, a motorway or a line speed above the usual limit. Such a crossing is possible with special safety measures (full barriers, obstacle detection, train protection that stops trains while it is not clear), so the design warns but does not reject it. Each level crossing also gives its position in degrees (`lonlat`) and a `road_closure`: how long one train at line speed closes the street, warning time + (rail zone + train length) / line speed + reopen time (barriers rising, or the intergreen of tram signals, `level_crossing.reopen_s`), with the mode's platform length as the train length, and the trains per hour assumed until a timetable exists (both directions, from the longest peak and off-peak headways `timetable.policy_headway_*_min`).
- **Street running**: where tram or light rail track embedded in the street runs along a street from OpenStreetMap (id `sr{track}-{way}-{n}`). Each lists the track, its chainage range and line sections, how the tram shares the street (`reserved`: its own lane with road traffic beside it; `mixed`: road traffic drives on the track, so the tram is delayed by it; `pedestrian_zone`), the road speed limit the tram keeps to (already in the sections' `speed_kmh`), the street (OSM way, name, class, width, lanes, one-way, buses, trolleybus wire) and the tram stops on the stretch.
- **Existing joins**: where a free end of new track joins an existing railway from OpenStreetMap. Each lists the track and which end (`0` start, `1` end), the `end` nodes there (one per line of the track), the OSM way, its name and `railway` kind, and whether the track continues the way at its end (`continues`) or needs a new turnout in the existing track. The existing network itself is not in the export.
- **Signals**: the planner's signal plan, or one generated from the design while the document has none (`signals_generated`). Each signal stands on a section at `chainage_m` from its `from` node. It governs trains moving towards the section's `to` node (`facing: "to"`) or towards its `from` node, and is a `main`, `block`, `shunting` or `distant` signal. `overlap_m` is the overlap beyond it (null where trains drive on sight). `lineside` is false for a block marker under cab signalling. Train detection follows from the signals and the switches' fouling points. Generated ids (`g{n}`) change between exports; planned ids (`sg{id}`) stay.
- **Depot groups**: connected storage sections.

Ids come from the document's ids, so they stay the same while the elements they come from stay (see the schema's descriptions). `valid` is false while the design has errors.

## Level crossing closures

`rail-plan` turns a planned day into the times each level crossing closes the street. The street closes when a train's head passes the approach point (`approach_m` before the crossing), or when it leaves a stop between the approach point and the crossing, but at least the warning time before it arrives. It opens `reopen_s` after the tail has cleared the rail zone beyond the crossing. Overlapping closures (trains meeting, or the two lines of a double track) are one closure. The ids are the topology's level crossing ids.

The street line tool reads the file (or, without one, the topology's `road_closure`) and adds the expected wait at each level crossing to the running time of every leg that passes it. A bus arriving at a random moment waits Σ c² / (2 T) on average for closures c in a period T, the uniform delay of a signal with no queue (HCM). Running times use the busiest clock hour of the timetable, or the assumed peak service.
